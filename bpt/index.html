Business, Product and Technology

Concept, the loop, domains, cycles, operation, the relation to organization planning

The BPT concept#

The whole operating model is divided into three decoupled “domains”: Business, Product and Technology. A domain name reflects its role. The domains are decoupled but act together, in what we call the “BPT Loop” constellation.

  • Each domain runs decoupled from the other two. Being decoupled, the three domains run in parallel. That follows from the decoupling itself and is an attribute of the operating model’s conceptual architecture.
  • Communication between domains is required, asynchronous and two-way; none of the domains waits for another.
    • Somewhat like “email based chat” (which we do not recommend for online collaboration).

The “core of the trick” is to consistently follow this simple concept when implementing it.

Continuous Product-Centric Cycle

The Product#

The “item” the BPT domains together deliver is a single Product. A Product is whatever the Business declares and Technology implements: a software product, a service, documentation, a process description, a physical book, and so on. A Product development exists in the documents and deliverables that are the outcome of a DBJ ADM wheel.

Not every kind of Product involves coding or engineering deliverables.

IMPORTANT
One product == one production line == one BPT instance.

A Product is fully described by 3 × 5 (15) deliverables: each of the three BPT domains runs one DBJ ADM wheel, and each wheel produces five deliverables. The deliverables all support the development of the same Product. Each is written in the context of the domain it resides in. In every domain, the work on the Product is a project of that domain. The deliverables are presumably small. Their existence and their classification is what matters.

The three domains#

Once the required Organisation Maturity (DBJ CMM, L3 to start, L5 the target) is established, the organization is ready to operate smoothly over three decoupled, interconnected, collaborating domains, each with a distinct mandate.

The Product domain is the alignment point between Business and Technology. It decouples each, so both can evolve independently, while coupling them, so delivery is coherent and traceable.

Domains are not software “silos”. They are organization units, made by people acting in their roles, clearly separated along the lines of clear cut responsibilities.

NOTE
Example: the Business Analyst (BA), a recommended role in the Product domain, is informed by Business domain deliverables on WHAT is required. The BA creates workflow diagrams to translate what into how, but only until the implementation takes over. How it is built is the responsibility of the Technology roles, operating in the Technology domain. Each of the roles can have other duties and “jurisdictions” completely encapsulated in their fields of expertise, as the business sees fit.

In smaller organizations adopting BPT, people in one role often wear multiple domain hats. Example: a single Product Owner (PO) can be involved in both the Business and the Product domain. DBJ Method requires that a person’s domain association is always explicit. Thanks to that:

Domain logical architecture#

  • A BPT domain is conceptually a service.
  • Each domain maintains its private persistent storage of deliverables, its logical repository.
  • Domain operations communicate with the adjacent domains. The Product domain communicates with two.

The DBJ ADM wheels are the logical domain interfaces. The wheel does not “somehow” hand deliverables to roles directly; it places them in the correct logical domain repository. Roles “watch” the domain repository, they never watch the wheel directly. This is what keeps the wheel and the domains decoupled.

The domains are operationally decoupled. They live and operate independently of each other, and can be logically STARTed and STOPped as a result of information arriving from an adjacent domain. That basically means “starting” and “stopping” the ADM wheels.

Exchange information with other domains, but keep the domain storage private.

DBJ ADM in every domain#

Each domain contains one DBJ ADM wheel. Each rotation of the wheel delivers exactly five deliverables. Technology is not an exception: it maintains its own wheel, primarily to be monitored and to offer the five structured ADM deliverables. The Technology ADM is the interface towards the Product domain.

DBJ Method encapsulates complex TOGAF artifacts behind this Operating Model, for feasible business usage. TOGAF gives structural integrity but stays underneath. Organization roles never see its complexity directly.

  • TOGAF ADM is the central structure for Enterprise Architecture projects, used by (many) Enterprise Architects producing only EA Artifacts, by default in large corporations.
  • DBJ Method has encapsulated the TOGAF ADM wheel into the DBJ ADM, a version in which the business truly drives the outcome. Good analogy: the steering wheel of the business ship.
  • At the helm of the DBJ ADM wheel is the business. Enterprise Architecture should be the navigator.
  • Business roles become accountable and responsible for the conceptual architecture by being onboarded onto the simplified Maturity Model, the DBJ CMM.

The BPT Operating Model is Architecture Governance as a productized execution model. It does not replace the ADM wheel: each domain turns its own wheel, producing the five deliverables that the adjacent domains consume.

Cycles#

The core operating rhythm of the DBJ Method. A cycle is the iteration between two adjacent domains, using ADM deliverables. These are not tight and fast project loops: BPT is composed of two sequential and decoupled cycles that logically connect the otherwise decoupled domains.

  • Cycle 1: B ↔ P: Business commissions; Product translates and returns for acceptance.
  • Cycle 2: P ↔ T: Product specifies; Technology builds and returns for verification.

A cycle is satisfied when the two adjacent domains agree. What is agreed depends on the domains and the kind of Product.

Each domain has explicit handoff points, emitting and receiving structured artifacts that ensure the continuity of the whole model. Except for Business, no domain finishes work without a clear input from the prior domain, and no domain publishes without producing an output defined by DBJ ADM.

Vocabulary#

Working increment#

The testable part of the Product that Technology builds and integrates against the Product specification, and returns to Product for verification. It is the outcome of the Technology phase “Build & Integrate” in Cycle 2.

UAT package#

What Product prepares so that Business can evaluate the Product meaningfully: the verified increment staged for acceptance, accompanied by the verification report and the UAT schedule. It is the outcome of the Product phase “Verify & Stage for UAT” in Cycle 2.

Both are operational outputs of a cycle. They are not the five DBJ ADM deliverables, which each domain’s wheel produces and stores on its own.

Change in mid-flight#

Business can change the project “mid-flight”. That questions the current Product existence, after the second cycle has started for the current Product.

  • In that scenario Product stops the second cycle until the first cycle is finished and Technology can restart the Product with a new set of requirements.
  • Business and Product decide on the cost of that operation.
    • If the change is ADD, the Product flows into the next version.
    • If the change is REPLACE, a Product fork is required. The current product goes into the deprecated state.

The four activity streams#

Activity streamIntentKey roles

DeployTechnology prepares the product for UAT; Product manages the UAT. After UAT is passed, Business gives final approval and the Product is released into operations. EA validates architecture complianceTechnology, Product, EA

The Evaluate stream feeds back to Business, so the next Require event can initiate the commencement. The loop never stops.

BPT Exceptions#

A BPT Exception is an human readable structure, thrown by a event by a domain team role.

It is not a technical defect report. A defect says the thing is broken. An exception says the thing was asked for wrongly, or understood wrongly, somewhere upstream, and that the asking is what has to be revisited. Example: Technical domain Customer error reports are transformed into BPT exceptions.

Exceptions flight path#

Exceptions travel against the flow of work, from the domain that catches the mismatch back towards the domain that caused it.

flowchart RL
    T(Technology) -->|Exception| P(Product)
    P -->|Exception| B(Business)

Origin of the Exception is Exception Event. Exception event most often happen inside the Technology domain. It can also happen inside the Product domain. It always sends the Exception to the previous domain.

  1. Technology catches it first. Engineering is where a mismatch becomes undeniable: what was specified cannot be built, or what was built cannot do what was asked. Most often what was built does not serve the customer. The exception gets “fired” back to Product with the full account of what happened.
    1. Usually implemented as some kind of simple messaging. Carrying the exception structure agreed in advance.
      1. Often that is a MD document, whose grey matter is a well know way to have simple and well known structure.
  2. Product reviews. Product either resolves it inside its own domain, because the specification was wrong and Product owns the specification, or it cannot, and the exception continues.
  3. Business is the final arbiter. An exception that reaches Business has arrived at the top accountable domain, where the product decision originated. There is nowhere further for it to go.

An BPT exception propagates with the full trace back: through the Product decisions, all the way to the Business decisions that started it.

Why the trace matters#

Without the trace, every failure looks like a Technology failure, because Technology is where it surfaced. The organization then fixes the symptom, at the cheapest place to blame and the most expensive place to change.

With the trace, the true origin is visible, and it frequently sits outside Technology altogether: a business decision whose reasoning nobody recorded, a project scoped against an assumption that has since been disproven.

That is why an exception reaching Business must land on something: the Business Decision Record that holds the why. Without it Exceptions flight path ends at a decision nobody can explain, and the exercise proves nothing.

BPT agility#

Backtracking is built into the operating model rather than bolted on as escalation. That is what makes the loop agile in the only sense that matters at scale: expensive mistakes are discovered at their source, while there is still something to correct.

NOTE
An exception is an event like any other in the loop. It is exchanged through the domain interfaces and recorded in the domain repositories, not carried by email.

Governance, roles and maturity#

Before starting the BPT journey, the organization team is trained in the best practices of CMM. Each domain should appoint a Domain Lead role, responsible for architecture decisions within the domain scope. Domain Leads co-own the BPT cycle governance and escalate cross-domain conflicts to shared resolution. Domain Leads should regularly cooperate with the Architecture authority.

Enterprise Architecture (EA), if present, governs the BPT loop from above. It does not participate in delivery. It defines the principles, governs the transitions, and measures alignment health through the feedback.

Each person holds proof of a passed DBJ CMM “L exam”. L3 is the required starting level and L5 the required target; see starting at L3. That is the precondition for smooth BPT cycling and delivery.

The BPT and the Organization#

This section is a logical view, at the organization level, of how business initiatives, product projects and technology projects relate. It is not part of the BPT or of the DBJ Method. It only shows how the organization’s planning maps onto an externally defined BPT.

Organization planning is external to the Factory, and it predates the BPT instances. Most organizations already have business initiatives, product projects and technology projects, named or not. An organization’s own layers (sometimes called “domains”) are partitioned in Business, Product and Technology. Those are organization layers, not BPT domains.

Projects in the organization’s planning are independent of each other: they are defined once and together, and immediately after that run separately, just as the organization layers themselves do. Information exchange is two-way throughout: during planning, development, and after deployment, all the way through the never-ending maintenance stage of the product. Enterprise Architecture (if present) oversees all initiatives and keeps them aligned.

IMPORTANT
Inside a BPT the Business domain contains projects only, never initiatives. A business initiative is an artifact of organization planning. One initiative can result in two or more products. The Business domain of a BPT develops exactly one product, and, if there is one, links to the Business Item deliverable of the organization planning. The connection between initiatives and BPT projects is made above the BPT, at the organization level.

One organization hosts one Product Factory, made of 1 to N BPT instances: one production line, one BPT instance, per Product. The factory scales horizontally, by adding one BPT instance per Product. Scalability is achieved by multiplying BPT instances.

Applicability#

Everything above is a logical view. It defines the communication, not the physical form.

  • Small organization, a single product. One BPT instance: one project in the Business domain passes information to one project in the Product domain. Once the Business ↔ Product iterations have settled, the Product project requires the Technology domain to organize and execute development and delivery.
  • Larger organization. 1 to N BPT instances in one Product Factory. How the organization plans across them, and how its initiatives are coordinated, for example in quarterly plans, is the organization’s own and is not prescribed by the method.
  • Physical form. On the physical level all the domains might be, for example, top level folders inside a single repository. For many organizations and products that is the right-sized BPT implementation. The domain might keep a simple folder and files structure, as ever before, for the five ADM deliverables.
  • Entry. Maturity before anything: the personal “L exam” is the entry ticket, L3 is the required start, and L5 is the required target.
